## 1. Scope and hypotheses

- Stage 1 compares **R**, the return objective of §2, against **R+D**, the two-stage preference of §2 that selects the most diversified (highest-DR²) admissible portfolio around R's selection, on **both** menus. The old fixed-risk ENB ladder is a descriptive reference only; neither arm may optimize it and relabel its output as return optimization.
- Stage 2 adds **R+E**, the same two-stage rule with ENB as the Stage B measure, iff C8 records **positive conditional-model evidence** for DR² in both menus under §7 (D7). Results that do not reach that label (no benefit, mixed/inconclusive results, insufficient power, an inactive preference) require **skipping ENB**; there is no separate-reason exception. This is the owner's computational stopping policy, not proof that DR² and ENB are mathematically equivalent or cannot ever add different value. Label skipped ENB **unevaluated**, not ineffective. "Confirmed worthwhile benefit" (realized benefit support in both menus and no material-harm risk cell, §7) is no longer the ENB gate; it remains the bar for any production default change.
- Start with History replay. Pure replay functions exist in main; numerical GARCH functions exist in the integration run, but their existence does not establish qualification. The common return optimizer and rolling harness are absent. GARCH needs a later separately locked replication using its real engine; do not mix it into this replay comparison or substitute a normal approximation (D10, unchanged).
- The experiment is offline and needs no API/UI changes. The deployable Phase 1 checkpoint follows C8. Holdings are engine-dependent by construction under D1: the selected engine supplies the B/T/G that Stage A and the guards rank. This experiment neither completes that checkpoint nor fixes the white-line choice (D9; design spec §5.5, deferred to C8). C8 decides the eventual delivery scope and reconciles affected assertions explicitly.
- Main hypotheses: DR² can improve conditional return outcomes or stability without material return harm; it can instead add no measurable value or create undesirable sacrifices. All are admissible results. No global optimum, universal diversification benefit or monotonic safer-direction control is assumed.
- **Mechanism (D6).** The MDP is the maximum-Sharpe portfolio only when expected excess returns are proportional to volatilities, so a DR² preference acts as a prior that discounts `mu_s`. DR² can improve realized outcomes only by (a) hedging estimation error in `mu_s`, or (b) delivering lower realized volatility at the same forecast return. Neither ENB nor DR² measures downside; Bad, ES and the loss probabilities do. A positive result is interpreted through (a) or (b), never as diversification improving returns by itself; §6 adds the diagnostic that correlates each origin's paired realized return difference with its `mu_s` forecast error and reports the sign.

## 2. Preference and materiality contract (owner policy, 2026-10-18)

Use unrounded annual simple returns in decimal units: `B=q(.025)`, `T=q(.50)`, `G=q(.975)`, with linear quantiles. One basis point (bp) is `0.0001`; one percentage point (pp) is `0.01`. Typical is the median, not the expected mean. Report mean separately.

**Return utility:** `U(w)=0.60 B(w)+0.30 T(w)+0.10 G(w)`. The same one-bp increase has strictly descending importance B, T, G. Locally, a one-bp Bad sacrifice requires more than two bp Typical or six bp Good to improve U. This is a finite tradeoff, not strict lexicographic maximization. For the owner's A `[-.05,.04,.12]` and B `[-.0501,.08,.18]`, `U(B)-U(A)=.01794` (1.794 pp), so B wins. There is no kink or stopping point at Bad = 0.

**Diversification measure (D5):** `DR²=(sum_i w_i sigma_i)^2/(w' Sigma w)`, with `sigma` and `Sigma` from the origin's training covariance, pinned to the production `estimate_moments` fixed-window estimator (`portfoliosight/engine/stats.py`): Ledoit–Wolf shrinkage on the standardized returns renormalized to a correlation matrix, times unshrunk `ddof=1` sample volatilities, `sigma_i = max(sqrt(252)·daily sd_i, 1e-4)`, `Sigma = outer(sigma, sigma)·corr`; recomputed at each origin from the truncated window (§4), with the fitted shrinkage intensity recorded per origin and menu in `search.parquet`. Two facts follow. DR² rises with correlation shrinkage toward the identity for long-only holdings whose pairwise correlations are non-negative; a negatively correlated pair can move the other way. The estimator is therefore part of the measure's definition, and a DR² value is not comparable across estimators. DR² is invariant to a zero-volatility holding (scaling the risky remainder toward it multiplies numerator and denominator by the same factor) and nearly so for a holding at the `1e-4` floor; on TSP it therefore cannot determine the G-fund weight, the risk ceiling does. "DR² did not move G" must not be read as a failure of the preference. Stage 2 substitutes `M=ENB` calculated by the existing minimum-torsion definition; disclose its known correlated-menu inflation.

**Two-stage preference (D1).** The financial feasible set is identical in every arm (§3). After searches, construct the same union candidate pool for all arms (§5) and remove candidates dominated on all three return quantiles. A candidate dominates another only when **no return quantile is lower at all** and at least one is higher by more than `1e-10`; this strict partial order prevents tolerance-induced cycles. Dominance uses the optimization bank only, not statistical significance or independent evaluation; `1e-10` is numerical resolution, not economic materiality. Then:

- **Stage A, return.** `w_R` is the maximum-U member of the filtered pool, selected by the tie-set rule below. R's selection is `w_R`.
- **Admissible set.** Relative to `w_R`, a pool member is admissible when `B >= B_R - 10 bp`, `T >= T_R - 25 bp`, `G >= G_R - 50 bp`, `U >= U_R - 2.5 bp` (the primary U cap) and it does not have both Bad and Typical lower than `w_R`'s by more than `1e-10` (the veto). Every guard bounds a **reduction** only; improvements in any outcome are uncapped and never count against a candidate. `w_R` always passes.

| Allowed reduction versus w_R | Maximum (owner policy 2026-10-18) |
|---|---:|
| Bad | 10 bp |
| Typical | 25 bp |
| Good | 50 bp |
| U | 2.5 bp (primary; §7 sweeps 0, 5, 10 bp) |
| Both Bad and Typical lower by more than `1e-10` | Never admissible |

- **Stage B, diversification.** R+D is the maximum-DR² member of the admissible set: "the most diversified admissible portfolio found". Ties within `1e-12` of the maximum DR² break by higher U, then B, T, G, then the lexicographically smaller weight vector in fixed menu order. Stage 2 applies the same rule with `M=ENB`.

The former additive score `U + 0.001·d(DR²)`, `d(M)=clip(log2 M,0,4)`, is deleted: at the 2.5 bp U cap a 19% DR² gain already outweighed the whole allowance, so the coefficient was inert and the cap was the real lever; the two-stage rule makes the cap the explicit control. A candidate dominated on the three return quantiles by another pool member is removed before either stage, so Stage B cannot select it however high its DR². These guards are an explicit part of the preference, not a new risk/cap constraint or a claim that DR² itself improves returns. They prevent diversification alone buying deterioration in both Bad and Typical. Report which guard binds, every accepted sacrifice, and the filtered pool's unconstrained maximum-DR² member beside the §5 MDP anchor. **Tie-set rule:** for each ranking, first compute its global maximum score, then form the tie set within `1e-12` of that maximum; select by higher B, then T, G, then lexicographically smaller weight vector in fixed menu order (Stage B prepends U to that order, as above). Apply this once to U for `w_R` and once to DR² (or ENB) for the Stage B selection; never use pairwise approximate-tie comparisons. The guards apply identically to R+E if authorized.

Positive return weights and Pareto filtering prefer simultaneous B/T/G improvements when the search finds them. A feasible candidate improving all four outcomes beats the incumbent; failure to find one does not prove impossibility. The owner adopted these coefficients, caps and tolerances on 2026-10-18; the accepted A/B example does not uniquely identify them, and they are policy, not derived quantities.

**Product semantics served (D9).** Per the design spec [§5.5](../specs/2026-10-14-portfoliosight-simulation-engines-design.md#55-selected-optimization-method-and-comparison), the dashboard's diversification slider becomes the allowed U sacrifice: left = `w_R` at zero sacrifice, right = the cap, each position selecting the most diversified admissible mix at that allowance; the default landing point is `w_R`; the per-quantile guards and the veto always apply; volatility is a ceiling, not an equality. Whether the white line ("Best return at each level of risk") and the Optimized point become the max-U mix `w_R` or stay the max-`mu_s` frontier is deferred to C8. The experiment produces arm selections at the evaluated allowances (the 2.5 bp primary cap and the §7 cap sweep), not the dashboard's 14-level product ladder; it therefore reports, per origin and ceiling, both the max-`mu_s` frontier mix and `w_R` with their B/T/G, U, `ES_2.5` and DR² (§6, §9), so C8 decides with evidence.

## 5. Reproducible broad search and local refinement

Use existing NumPy/SciPy/CVXPY/Clarabel dependencies only, with exact versions recorded and BLAS restricted to one thread. No new dependency is authorized. Algorithm:

1. For each `(menu,origin,ceiling,replicate)`, create one 2,000 × 252 stationary replay index bank, mean block length 21, shared by all candidate evaluations and arms. Recenter each portfolio's historical daily returns on `mu_s'w/252` exactly as production; retain production's unscaled sample volatility and linear percentiles. Use the count-matrix identity for speed, validated against explicit daily compounding. Do not replace the objective with mean-minus-volatility, Gaussian quantiles or an annual buy-and-hold surrogate.
2. Use five complete optimization replicates, path seeds `20261013..20261017`. Candidate generator seeds are the corresponding seed plus `1_000_000`. Initialize a new seeded generator in each cell; identical seeds deliberately provide common random numbers across arms. Record the full generated matrices' hashes. **Primary conditional-policy return statistics and realized comparisons use optimizer seed `20261013` only.** The other four optimizer seeds are stability replications and per-seed diagnostics, never extra historical N, pooled policy-return observations or opportunities to choose a better policy.
3. Shared anchors: minimum variance, feasible cap-projected equal weight, the expected-return maximizers (the max-`mu_s` frontier mixes) at X(.25), X(.50), X(1), and the return-agnostic most-diversified portfolio (MDP, D3) at the same ceilings, `maximize sum_i sigma_i w_i s.t. sqrt(w' Sigma w) <= X, sum(w)=1, caps`, the design spec §5.2 program with its return floor dropped (Choueifaty & Coignard 2008, *J. Portfolio Management* 35(1)), all restricted to ceilings at/below the current X. The MDP program is convex (CVXPY/Clarabel) and its solution has the largest `sum_i sigma_i w_i` of any feasible portfolio at that ceiling; where its risk constraint does not bind, record its actual risk and DR² rather than asserting it is the maximum-DR² point at X (spec §5.2's off-line case). Retain duplicate attempts in the budget ledger, but cache duplicate evaluations. The legacy ENB ladder is never a search seed or optimizer substitute.
4. Generate 1,024 scrambled Sobol points in dimension `N+1` using a complete power-of-two draw. For even row indices, transform first N coordinates by `-log(clip(u,2^-53,1-2^-53))`, normalize, and Euclidean-project to the capped simplex. For odd rows, greedily fill the caps in descending coordinate order, ties by fund order, producing sparse boundary candidates. For each row, mix its candidate toward `w_min` by the last coordinate's square root; if risk exceeds X, move along the segment toward `w_min` by 60 bisection steps until feasible. Capped-simplex projection solves `sum clip(z_i-lambda,0,cap_i)=1` by 80 bisection steps, bracket `[min(z)-1,max(z)]`. Reject failed feasibility rather than normalize a violation away. These transformed points provide broad interior/boundary coverage; they are not a uniform feasible-set sample or a global-optimality certificate. [SciPy's Sobol documentation](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.stats.qmc.Sobol.html) motivates the complete power-of-two draw.
5. Select six shared local starts from the broad pool by descending U, greedily retaining starts at L1 distance >= .10 from every already selected start; if fewer than six qualify, fill from the remaining U order. If fewer than six distinct weights exist, repeat starts with separately recorded budgets. Each arm refines those same starts with its own objective. R maximizes U. R+D maximizes DR² subject to the §2 admissibility guards, taken relative to the maximum-U member of the shared broad archive (known to both arms before either local search, so neither arm needs the other's cache or results), as explicit inequality constraints; a feasible candidate that violates a guard still enters the feasible archive with its guard flags, because admissibility is finally judged against the union `w_R` in step 7. R+E, if authorized, maximizes ENB the same way. Both arms use COBYLA in N-1 coordinates with the final weight `1-sum(first N-1)`, all bounds and the risk inequality explicit, `rhobeg=.05`, `tol=1e-5`, `catol=1e-8`, `maxiter=384`. An external counter limits **all objective calls**, including infeasible/repeated calls, to 384 per start. Every candidate crossing a bound, sum or risk feasibility check by more than `1e-8` receives a deterministic penalty `1e6+1e6*sum(positive constraint violations)` and cannot enter the feasible pool. Define actual recentered replay growth `g_t=1+r_t'w-mean_s(r_s'w)+(mu_s'w)/252`, with the mean over training rows. Any `g_t<=0` receives the same base `1e6` minimization penalty, is excluded from the feasible archive, and retains the named `nonpositive_daily_growth` status, date and weights; do not clip it. Raw-input validity is a separate data check, not a substitute for this recentered-growth check. Invalid proposals count toward the call cap and invalid-proposal rate, not as whole-arm failures. SciPy documents `maxiter` as COBYLA's function-evaluation cap and warns that its trust-region tolerance is not an accuracy guarantee. [COBYLA reference](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/optimize.minimize-cobyla.html)
6. Broad proposals (1,024 plus at most eight anchors) and local budgets (six × 384 = 2,304 calls **per arm**) are identical across arms. Quantiles change nonsmoothly when path rankings change; local refinement is a bounded heuristic. Record solver status, feasible evaluations and best-score trace even if the solver reports success. Never grant a losing arm extra starts or tune tolerances after seeing comparisons.
7. Union the feasible broad and both arms' local candidates, rescore each using the same cached return vector and covariance, then apply §2's dominance filter, Stage A (`w_R`), admissible set and Stage B selection. This gives both arms equal access to discoveries and prevents claiming a DR² benefit caused merely by a worse return-only search. Report native-search selections (each arm's two-stage result on its own archive) as diagnostics, final union selections as the primary comparison, and the shared versus arm-specific work separately. The union protocol is a research comparison, not a measured production architecture. If Stage 2 is later authorized, use the same per-arm budgets for all three arms and a three-arm union; preserve the original Stage-1 verdict and label any changed R/DR selections as Stage-2 pool-enrichment effects.
8. Lock selected weights before examining any independent draws or realized returns. Evaluate each optimizer seed's fixed selections on five **new** replay banks with seeds `20261113..20261117`, 2,000 paths each. For primary optimizer seed `20261013`, compute primary test quantiles from its pooled 10,000 annual outcomes; retain separate bank-level and optimizer-seed diagnostics. Never pool outcomes across optimizer seeds into the primary policy statistic. These draws test selection sensitivity conditional on the same historical sample/model; they are not new market history or realized out-of-sample returns. Do not replace a selected portfolio with one chosen from this test bank.

**Failure taxonomy and denominator:** evaluation-budget exhaustion with a valid feasible archive is normal bounded-search completion. A failed local start (solver returns non-success and no usable refinement) is recorded separately; other starts and the unchanged valid broad archive remain available. Ordinary invalid proposals receive the stated penalty, consume calls and retain raw statuses; they are not failed starts or arms. A failed arm is an inability to emit a valid final selection from that archive or a named computational error in its required evaluation. Verify `w_min` is feasible and has `g_t>0` for every recentered training row before searches; its retained archive entry guarantees an admissible maximum-U anchor unless a true input/computation error occurs. Verify each MDP anchor is feasible (caps, sum and ceiling within `1e-8`) before searches; a failed or infeasible MDP solve is a named computational error for that cell, never a silently dropped anchor. ## 6. Measurements and dependence-aware evidence

For every selected portfolio, retain training and independent-bank B/T/G, U, `ES_2.5`, expected mean, annual path mean, loss probability, annual-loss-beyond-20% probability, raw DR², ENB only if Stage 2 is authorized, risk, cap/sum residuals, active guards, and exact weights. Report whether all three returns improve, whether all four improve, and each signed sacrifice; a bigger diversification number is never the benefit verdict by itself.

**Expected shortfall (D4).** `ES_2.5` is the mean of the worst `ceil(0.025·P)` annual outcomes of a bank (50 of 2,000; 250 of the pooled 10,000), retained for training and independent banks and reported as paired ΔES per menu and risk cell. It is a diagnostic, not a guard: two portfolios with equal Bad can have very different ES, and U does not see it. Promotion to a guard needs a recorded amendment.

**References (D3).** Beside each cell's R and R+D, report three return-agnostic references with the same retained measures: minimum variance, cap-projected equal weight and the MDP at that ceiling (§5 step 3). Report R against those three first: the return optimizer must itself be validated against return-agnostic choices before R+D versus R is interpreted. The MDP's published record, stated so that it is not re-litigated here: in Choueifaty & Coignard (2008) the Eurozone MDP beat the best of cap-weight, minimum-variance and equal-weight by +1.8/+2.2/+1.4 pp annualized return over the full period/1992–2000/2001–2008; the US MDP by +0.7 pp over the full period, −3.7 pp in 1992–2000 (with lower Sharpe), +4.7 pp in 2001–2008. The paper's headline claim is Sharpe ratio, its benchmarks are all return-agnostic, equities only, 1992–February 2008. None of that is evidence about a return-forecast-aware optimizer on these menus.

**Frontier and `w_R` per origin (D9).** For each origin and ceiling, report the max-`mu_s` frontier mix (the §5 step 3 expected-return maximizer) and `w_R`, each with weights, risk, B/T/G, U, `ES_2.5` and DR² on the training and independent banks; C8 decides from this whether the white line ("Best return at each level of risk") and the Optimized point become max-U or stay max-`mu_s`.

Realized evidence uses each origin's actual following 252 daily returns: annual return, loss indicator, return below -20%, worst daily return, annual maximum drawdown and realized annualized daily volatility. Report per-arm empirical q(.025)/q(.50)/q(.975) across origins and their U, plus paired annual-return differences, loss rates and drawdowns. There is **one realized annual return per origin**, not a realized q(.025) at every origin. Monthly cohorts overlap heavily; risk ceilings and menus also share outcomes. Neither those cells nor simulated paths are independent historical replications.

**Forecast-error diagnostic (D6).** For each mature origin compute `w_R`'s `mu_s` forecast error, the realized annual return minus the forecast `mu_s'w_R`, and correlate it across origins with the paired realized annual-return difference (R+D minus R; the per-origin realized counterpart of ΔU), per menu and risk cell. Report the sign and a descriptive block-bootstrap interval. A negative correlation (R+D helps most where `mu_s` overshot) is mechanism (a) of §1; the diagnostic interprets a result and is not a gate.

Calibration is separate: for each q in {.025,.50,.975}, report `1[y<=forecast_q]`, nominal versus empirical coverage, and pinball loss `(q-1[y<forecast_q])*(y-forecast_q)`. It assesses forecasts of each selected portfolio, while policy-performance comparisons use actual returns/drawdowns above. Lower pinball loss for a near-cash allocation does not establish a better portfolio: changing holdings changes the target distribution. Do not select R+D because its calibration loss is smaller. Proper scoring concerns forecast honesty, not the owner's utility. [Gneiting and Raftery (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000001437)

Stability: compute once across the five optimizer seeds for each menu/origin/ceiling (not once as a vote per test bank); report median and 90th-percentile pairwise weight L1 distances, standard deviation across optimizer seeds of each seed's pooled-test B/T/G and U, selection/guard frequencies, and adjacent-origin target turnover `0.5*L1(w_t-w_previous)`. Also evaluate fixed primary weights on each independent bank to separate Monte Carlo variation from reoptimization. Adjacent-origin turnover is a policy-change diagnostic, not frictionless-rebalancing trading volume.

Aggregate in this fixed order: first equal weight the three prescribed risk cells within an origin, then equal weight origins within a menu, then average the two menu summaries 50/50. Keep each menu/risk table visible; aggregate success cannot mask a failing menu or risk cell. For nonlinear metrics, compute quantiles within each menu/risk cell across its origin returns before averaging the resulting contrasts. Do not concatenate the larger TSP panel or all simulated paths into a claimed larger historical sample.

Use a paired circular moving-block bootstrap over the chronological **monthly origin index**, block length 24 origins, 2,000 resamples, seed `20261201`; sample identical index blocks for every arm, risk cell and menu and recompute the complete statistic. Report percentile intervals, disjoint-interval counts, calendar span, missingness and sensitivity to lengths 12 and 36. These intervals remain exploratory with fewer than 20 disjoint annual intervals; block resampling cannot create missing regimes or independence. Do not use IID t-tests, binomial intervals based on overlapping months, or label bootstrap resamples additional evidence.

Tail-power rule: empirical 2.5% quantiles with fewer than 200 disjoint annual intervals have fewer than five expected tail observations. This floor is a warning threshold, not a guarantee of precision even above it. Below it, mark realized q(.025) superiority/calibration **underpowered**, display raw outcomes and ranges of plausible effects, and prohibit a confirmed real-world tail-benefit claim. The present Example history makes that limitation unavoidable; even the full TSP history is modest.
