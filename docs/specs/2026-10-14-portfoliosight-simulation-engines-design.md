## 1. Purpose

The page shows, for every mix, a one-year Bad / Typical / Good range and a diversification word. Both numbers come from one place each: the range from `portfoliosight.engine.ranges.one_year_ranges`, the word from the minimum-torsion effective number of bets (ENB). This spec replaces the single range method with three selectable engines, fixes a measurement problem in the diversification number, and sets out how we decide when a new engine is good enough to become the default.

The owner's goal is a copula simulation that measures the cross-dependence of the whole portfolio, especially in bad years. The GARCH-t filtered historical simulation (FHS) is the prudent first step toward it: it builds the volatility model and the shock pool that the copula then replaces through one seam.

## 2. The finding: minimum-torsion bets are inflated on a correlated menu

Measured on 2026-10-14 on the live 5-year tensors:

- Example Portfolio (23 funds): holding ONE fund alone scores 3.5 to 14.4 bets (median 10.0; VBIAX alone 14.4). Every one of the 672 ladder mixes scores above the number of funds it holds (weights above 0.5%); mixes holding 4 funds score 9 to 12.6.
- TSP (5 funds): single funds score 1.0 to 2.5.

Cause. The torsion is computed from the return covariance alone, with no look-through to holdings. On a menu of highly correlated funds the minimum-torsion factors sit far from the funds themselves, so one fund's risk spreads over many factors and a one-fund portfolio looks like many bets. The measure is a valid decomposition of variance (the shares sum to 1), but it does not answer the visitor's question "how many separate things am I betting on". The ladder optimizer (demo spec §4.7) maximizes this inflated number, so the map, the word and the Optimized point inherit the problem.

Holdings look-through (counting the stocks and bonds behind the funds) would answer the question directly, but none of the current data sources provides fund holdings; it is parked on the roadmap.

## 4. Engines

### 4.1 Common frame

Every engine produces, for a mix `w`, `P = 2,000` one-year paths of `H = 252` daily simple returns, compounds each path (`Y_p = ∏_t (1 + y_{p,t}) − 1`) and reports the 2.5th, 50th and 97.5th percentiles (linear interpolation) as Bad, Typical, Good, plus the engine-independent normal range `μ ± 1.96σ` (details only, "textbook bell-curve figure"). From 2026-10-18 (§3 item 7, D5) it also reports the **expected shortfall at 2.5%**, `ES_2.5 = mean of the ⌈0.025 P⌉ = 50 lowest Y_p` **[choice]** (the same tail Bad sits in, so the two figures read together: `ES_2.5 ≤ Bad` always; for a constant portfolio `ES_2.5 = Bad = Typical = Good`; the ENLC bad-year set of §5.3 is a different, wider tail at 5% and is unchanged). `ES_2.5` is a details-only diagnostic: never a headline, never a guard of §5.5. Output shape and names are those of `one_year_ranges` today, with the additions of §7.

| id | Settings label | shocks | volatility | dependence between funds |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `replay` | History replay | runs of real days from the lookback sample | the sample's own | the sample's own days |
| `garch` | Volatility model | real standardized residual rows, resampled one day at a time | fitted GARCH(1,1)-t per fund, long-run start | the residual rows (empirical) |
| `copula` | Copula | drawn from a fitted vine copula with fat-tailed margins | same as `garch` | fitted pair copulas (tail dependence beyond the history) |

Shared draws, so mixes are comparable. Within a build, every mix (frontier nodes, every candidate the §5.5 search evaluates under an engine, both variants' ladder levels, the entered mix, and later any mix asked through `/v1/range`) uses one draw per engine: `replay` the existing day-index matrix (`bootstrap_index`, seed `20261013`); `garch` one pool-row matrix `I_pool` (`P × H`, QSE `draw_row_indices`, seed `20261013`); `copula` one shock tensor `Z` (`P × H × N`, seed `20261013`). Both optimization variants use the same included funds, generation/date, lookback moments, bounds, input digests and versions. Reuse each shape fit, draw and simulated panel across variants; do not fit or simulate again for the other optimizer. Two mixes under one engine differ by their weights alone, so identical weights give identical ranges regardless of which optimizer found them. Across engines the paths are different objects (different mechanisms), so engines are compared on their percentiles, never path by path.

Determinism. BLAS on one thread during a build (as today); the GARCH fit is deterministic given the series and the pinned `arch` version; the vine fit is deterministic given the data, the family set and the pinned `pyvinecopulib` version when run single-threaded (`FitControls(num_threads=1)`); all seeds fixed. Building twice from the same price records, `D_G`, bounds and versions gives identical canonical JSON (the demo spec's §4.12 test, extended to every engine). Each engine's parameters and library versions are recorded in the tensor (§7) so a figure can always be traced to its method.

### 4.2 History replay (unchanged)

The stationary block bootstrap of demo spec §4.9: the mix's daily returns over the lookback sample, recentred on `μ_s'w / 252`, resampled in runs of mean length 21 days, 2,000 paths. Its volatility is not rescaled (the sample's own, which is the lookback's up to the correlation shrinkage). **[choice]** It keeps its exact current definition so the default engine's figures do not move under the owner while the others are validated; whether it should also take the §4.5 volatility step is decision 7, §13.

### 4.3 Volatility model: GARCH(1,1)-t filtered historical simulation

**Shape panel.** For the funds included in the window (demo spec §4.2 rule; a fund set aside for the lookback is not in the panel), the last `S = 2,520` rows of the calendar ending at `D_G` (the union of the included funds' trading days): each fund's daily total returns on its own available rows, `NaN` elsewhere. A fund with less than ten years contributes what it has. The panel is the same for every lookback whose included fund set is the same; its digest (`shape.sample_sha256`, the §4.9 `sample_digest` with `NaN` encoded as the canonical quiet NaN) is stored.

**Fit.** `qse.distributions.joint.fit_garch_t_panel(log1p(panel))`: per fund, on its daily log returns `ℓ_t = ln(1 + r_t)` (a missing return stays missing), by maximum likelihood on its non-missing rows,

```
ℓ_t = μ + ε_t,   ε_t = σ_t z_t,   σ²_t = ω + α ε²_{t−1} + β σ²_{t−1},   z_t ~ t_ν scaled to unit variance
```

returning `(μ, ω, α, β, ν)` in the units of the log returns (QSE's fitter lets `arch` rescale the series for its optimizer and converts the parameters back; nothing is scaled here), the conditional volatility `σ_t`, the standardized residuals `z_t = ε_t / σ_t` on the fund's rows, and a status. The fund's own full history fits its volatility persistence and tail; only the cross-fund dependence is limited to the overlap. **[choice]** Log returns (`method.returns = "log"`): a simulated simple return can fall below −100%, a log return cannot. TSP C's ten-year fit in simple returns (`α = 0.159`, `β = 0.837`, `ν = 5.5`) put 4 path-days at or below −100% (worst −228%) on 8 of 2,000 paths at 10y (measured 2026-10-17), so no mix holding it could be compounded; fitted in log returns, its worst simulated day is −91%. The panel and its digest stay the simple total returns. A day at or below −100% has no log return and fails the build with the fund and date named. The fallbacks below, the residuals, the pool, the splice guard and the outliers all read the log-space fit (the `near_constant` threshold applies to the log series).

**Fallbacks** (QSE's rules, adopted as they are; the tensor records the status of every fund):

| status | rule | simulated as |
|---|---|---|
| `garch` | fit converged, parameters valid | the recursion above |
| `insufficient_history` | fewer than 252 rows | constant volatility: `α = β = 0`, `ω` = sample variance, `z = (ℓ − mean) / sd` |
| `near_constant` | daily sd below `3e-4` (about 0.5%/yr): TSP G (0.3%/yr) always; SGOV, BIL and SHV sit near the line and fall on either side by window, so the rule decides and the tensor names them | constant volatility as above; sd at or below `1e-12` means zero residuals |
| `fit_failed` | optimizer failed, parameters invalid or non-finite | constant volatility |
| `non_stationary` | `α + β ≥ 1 − 1e-4` (no long-run variance) | constant volatility |

A fallback fund goes through the same recursion with its shock merely scaled, so every fund takes part in the joint rows. A fund so young that it has only 227 to 251 rows of history (the §4.2 start rule lets it into the 1-year view) is a constant-volatility fund; the Settings excluded-fund list is not involved, but the details view names fallback funds ("volatility held constant: TSP G, SGOV").

**Residual pool.** The rows on which every included fund has a residual (QSE's "common rows"), in panel order; each column re-standardized to mean 0 and variance 1 (`ddof = 0`; QSE `restandardize=True`), which leaves ranks, correlations and the empirical copula of the rows unchanged and makes the simulated unconditional mean and variance the model's in expectation (§4.5 then pins every mix's exactly). The pool contains the lookback sample's rows (the panel spans the window and residuals exist wherever returns do), so it has at least the window's 126 rows; a pool below 126 rows marks the engine `unavailable` for that tensor with the reason "not enough shared history for the volatility model" and the page disables it. QSE's own floor of 60 rows is never relied on. A short pool (a menu holding a young fund) identifies joint tails poorly; the tensor's `shape.pool_rows` says how many rows there are and the details view shows it.

**Splice guard.** The store's total-return index of a seeded fund is the lake's back-adjusted history spliced with the provider's tail at `adjusted_through` (demo spec §5.1). A missing corporate action is fund-specific; a crash is cross-sectional. So a fund whose standardized residual on its own splice date has `|z_n| > 6` **and** sits more than 6 away from the median residual of the other included funds that day (`|z_n − median_m z_m| > 6`) fails the build with the fund and date named (nothing is clipped). `|z| > 6` on any other date, or on the splice date with the rest of the menu moving too, is recorded in `shape.outliers` (fund, date, `z`) for the validation report and fails nothing. **Owner clarification, 2026-10-17:** if no other included fund has a residual on the splice date, the peer comparison is unavailable: retain the extreme and disclose it in `shape.outliers`; never substitute a fabricated peer median. A missing small dividend (a 1% drop on a fund with 1% daily volatility is `z ≈ −1`) passes this guard; the provider cross-check of demo spec §4.1 is the defence there.

**Simulation.** `simulate_fhs_panel(fit, P, H, row_indices=I_pool, start="long_run")` gives the daily log returns `ℓ` of shape `P × H × N`: at every (path, day) one pool row is drawn and that whole row is the day's shock for every fund, so funds keep moving together as they did on real days; each fund runs its own recursion from its long-run variance `ω / (1 − α − β)`. Memory: `2,000 × 252 × 23 × 8` bytes is 93 MB (30 funds: 121 MB); QSE measured a 126 MB peak at 30 funds on 2 CPUs in 0.14 to 0.22 s. The fitted drift `μ` and the long-run variance are replaced by the level step (§4.5), so the simulation supplies shape only.

### 4.4 Copula

The same fit, pool and recursion as §4.3; only the source of the shock rows changes, through `simulate_fhs_panel(..., shock_source=...)`.

**Margins.** For fund `n` the margin of its shock is the empirical distribution of its pool column (the quantile function by linear interpolation between order statistics), which is fat-tailed because the column is the fund's GARCH-t standardized residuals **[choice]**: with the same margins as `garch`, the two engines differ in the dependence model and nothing else, so the validation isolates what the copula adds; and a parametric Student-t margin with the fitted `ν` would, over the `P·H = 504,000` draws of a build, produce a largest shock of about 75 standard deviations at `ν = 2.5` and still 25 at `ν = 4`, a day at or below −100% on a single-fund mix, which raises. A Student-t margin with `ν` floored at 4 is decision 11, §13, as a later option. A zero-residual fund (status `near_constant` with zero variance) keeps a zero shock and is left out of the dependence fit. Pseudo-observations for the dependence fit: `u_{t,n} = rank(z_{t,n}) / (T_pool + 1)`. The margin type per fund is recorded (`shape.funds[].margin`: `empirical` or `zero`).

**Dependence.** A regular vine copula (R-vine) fitted on the pseudo-observations: structure by Dissmann's algorithm, pair-copula family per edge chosen by BIC from {Gaussian, Student-t, Clayton, Gumbel, Frank, Joe, BB1, BB7, each with its rotations}, truncated after tree 5 **[choice]** (on a 23- to 30-fund menu the deeper trees are close to independence and cost most of the fit time). The share of the fitted log-likelihood carried by each of the five trees is recorded (`shape.tree_loglik_share`) so the truncation level can be judged from data. Library: `pyvinecopulib` (C++ core, MIT licence; Phase 3 verifies a Python 3.12 Linux wheel and pins the version). The copula code lives in QSE (proposed module `qse.distributions.joint.copula`, a `ShockSource` factory plus the margin transforms), reviewed and merged there, then pinned here (owner decision 3).

**Simulation.** Draw `U` (`P·H` rows × `N`) from the vine with the fixed seed, map each column through its margin's quantile function to `Z` (`P × H × N`), and hand `Z` to the recursion as the shock source. Everything after that (recursion, level step, compounding, percentiles) is identical to §4.3, so the difference between the two engines is exactly the dependence model.

What the copula adds over the row bootstrap: joint bad days the history does not contain (a 2,520-row pool holds at most 126 rows in its worst 5%; the tail-dependent families extrapolate beyond them); a smoother tail for the loss-contribution measure (§5.3); and a dependence summary the owner asked for: for every pair of funds the lower-tail concordance of the simulated shocks at 5% (the probability that one fund is in its worst 5% of days given the other is; a finite-level figure, not the limit tail-dependence coefficient), stored as `shape.lower_tail_5pct` (N × N; `null` unless the tensor carries `copula`) for the validation report.

### 4.5 Level and shape: the scaling formulas

Chart moments for the selected lookback (demo spec §4.3): per fund the blended expected return `μ_{s,i}` and volatility `σ_i`; for a mix `μ_w = μ_s'w`, `σ_w = √(w'Σw)`. Daily targets invert the chart's annualization: `m_i = μ_{s,i} / 252`, `s_i = σ_i / √252`, `m_w = μ_w / 252`, `s_w = σ_w / √252`.

Step 1, per fund, in log space (level of each fund): with `ℓ̄_n` and `sd_n` the mean and population standard deviation of fund `n`'s log returns over all paths and days of the simulated panel, and `(m̃_n, s̃_n)` the log-return equivalents of the daily targets by lognormal moment matching,

```
s̃²_n = ln(1 + s²_n / (1 + m_n)²),   m̃_n = ln(1 + m_n) − s̃²_n / 2
ℓ'_{p,t,n} = m̃_n + (ℓ_{p,t,n} − ℓ̄_n) · s̃_n / sd_n,   R'_{p,t,n} = e^{ℓ'_{p,t,n}} − 1 > −1
                                                     (a constant fund, sd_n ≤ rounding: R' = m_n exactly)
```

Step 2, per mix, in simple space (level of the mix): `y_{p,t} = Σ_n w_n R'_{p,t,n}`; with `R̄'` and `Σ_R'` the pooled mean vector and (`ddof = 0`) covariance of `R'` over all paths and days, computed once per (fund set, engine, lookback), `ȳ = w·R̄'` and `sd(y)² = w' Σ_R' w`,

```
y'_{p,t} = m_w + k_w (y_{p,t} − ȳ),   k_w = s_w / sd(y)        (sd(y) = 0: y' = m_w)
```

so every mix's days have pooled mean `m_w` and standard deviation `s_w` exactly, and `k_w` needs no pass over the paths. `k_w ≠ 1` because the simulated correlations are the shape window's and the chart's risk uses the lookback's shrunk ones. **[choice]** The scale `sd_n` is the pooled standard deviation (`method.scale = "sd"`). It is not robust: on TSP's log-space fit, leaving out the 20 paths (1%) with the largest excursion moves C's `sd_n` by 18% (S 5%, I 3.5%, F 2.5%; the fixture's 23 funds under 0.1%), because on a few paths the fitted volatility runs away (C: 14 path-days on one path above 10 times its largest in-sample conditional volatility; measured 2026-10-17). An IQR-matched scale was measured and rejected: it narrowed C's Bad year further (−15.6% against −19.7%; −23.4% with those 20 paths left out), because step 2 pins each mix's spread over all paths, runaway ones included. Only a cap on the simulated variance undoes that, and QSE's recursion has none yet.

The year is `Y_p = ∏_t (1 + y'_{p,t}) − 1`. Under `replay` a day at or below −100% raises, as today, and no such mix enters a tensor (its days are real days). Under `garch` (and later `copula`) `R' > −1` always, but `k_w > 1` can still take a day of a mix to or below −100%; such a path is absorbed: its year is −100% (`method.ruin = "absorb"`), and the tensor records `engines.garch.ruin_paths`, the largest number of absorbed paths over every mix it carries, 0 normally. Implementation note: `R'` is a per-fund non-linear function of the raw simulated panel, which is the only large array; `R'` is computed per chunk of paths when needed (twice for its moments, once for the years), and the 673 mixes of a tensor are compounded in blocks over those chunks with work buffers of at most 64 MB in all, never all at once (which would be 2.7 GB). Typical sits near `e^{μ_w − σ_w²/2} − 1`, and the existing check (Typical within `0.25σ` of that figure) applies to every engine.

**[choice]** Two steps rather than QSE's one-step `rescale_to_targets` on the mix series: the one-step map weights funds by their ten-year volatilities inside the mix; step 1 gives each fund the lookback's own volatility before mixing (a bond fund in a 1-year view carries the weight the chart gives it) and step 2 pins the mix's risk to the chart's. The two-step form also decomposes exactly by fund, which §5.3 needs: `y'_{p,t} = Σ_n w_n [m_n + k_w (R'_{p,t,n} − R̄'_n)]`.

For `ew` the level is the weighted `μ_s`, `σ` and the shape is the same ten-year fit (the tensor's note says so). For `replay`, `R'` is the resampled sample row recentred per fund (`sample_{t,n} − mean_n + m_n`) and `k_w = 1`, which is what the engine does today.

## 5. Diversification measures

Three candidates, each stated with its definition, its value for a single fund, its bound, how it is computed from each engine, and its role in the §5.5 contract. The fixed-risk ladder program (demo spec §4.7), which maximized a measure at `σ(w) = X_j` exactly, is historical from 2026-10-18 (§3 item 7, D2): the solver descriptions below are kept because the programs survive as candidate generators and as the experiment's descriptive reference, not because they are the product's objective.

### 5.1 Minimum-torsion ENB (today's measure; kept as a baseline)

`ENB(w) = exp(−Σ_i p_i ln p_i)`, `p_i = Λ_i b_i² / (w'Σw)`, `b = T^{−T} w` (demo spec §4.6). Single fund: not 1 (3.5 to 14.4 on the Example Portfolio, §2). Bound: `ENB ≤ N`, the menu size, not the number of funds held. Computed from `Σ` alone: identical under every engine. Optimization, historical (the fixed-risk ladder, product behaviour until 2026-10-18): non-convex; SLSQP from several starts, about 2,000 programs per lookback (most of the 4 seconds of a 23-fund build); "the most diversified mix found". Role from 2026-10-18: a Stage B measure of §5.5, maximized over the admissible set (still non-convex, still a search, still "found"); the fixed-risk program is one of the pool's candidate generators. The browser computes it for any `w` from the shipped torsion.

### 5.2 Diversification ratio squared, DR²

`DR(w) = (Σ_i w_i σ_i) / √(w'Σw)` (Choueifaty & Coignard 2008, *J. Portfolio Management* 35(1)); the measure is `DR²`. Single fund: exactly 1. Bound: when every pairwise correlation among the funds held is non-negative, `DR² ≤` the number of funds held (Cauchy–Schwarz: `w'Σw ≥ Σ w_i²σ_i² ≥ (Σ w_iσ_i)² / k`); `k` uncorrelated funds at inverse-volatility weights score exactly `k`. With a negative correlation it can exceed `k` (a hedged pair is less than two risks, not more); the §10 check therefore uses the correlations among the funds each mix holds, and the tensor records the menu's lowest pairwise correlation (`min_pair_correlation`) for the report. Two funds at correlation 0.95, equal weights, equal volatility: `DR² = 1.03`, which is the behaviour the finding calls for. Computed from `Σ` alone: identical under every engine; the browser computes it from the `sigma` and `cov` it already has.

**Covariance (owner decision D5, §3 item 7).** `Σ` and `σ_i` are the production `estimate_moments` estimator (`portfoliosight/engine/stats.py`): sample volatilities (`ddof = 1`, floored at `SIGMA_FLOOR`) times a Ledoit–Wolf shrunk correlation (shrinkage estimated on the standardized returns and renormalized to a correlation matrix; for the exponentially weighted lookback the fixed `R_s = 0.8 R + 0.2 I`). Three consequences the report and the tests state rather than discover: (1) shrinkage pulls off-diagonal correlations toward zero, which leaves the numerator `Σ_i w_i σ_i` untouched; **DR² rises with correlation shrinkage toward the identity for long-only holdings whose pairwise correlations are non-negative; a negatively correlated pair can move the other way** (shrinking a negative correlation toward zero raises `w'Σw`), so for such holdings a DR² from the raw sample covariance is at most the tensor's for the same weights, and where a held pair is negatively correlated the report states the sign it observes rather than assuming it; (2) a fund with `σ_i = 0` contributes nothing to numerator or denominator, so **DR² is invariant to its weight**: DR² cannot set the weight of such a fund, the risk ceiling and the §5.5 guards do (TSP G, at 0.3%/yr, is near enough to zero that DR² is nearly indifferent to it: at the cap, G's weight is whatever the ceiling leaves); (3) DR² is a ratio of degree-2 forms, so it is also invariant to scaling the risky weights together, which is the same statement as (2) from the other side.

Optimization at fixed risk `X_j` and return floor `Y`, historical (product behaviour planned 2026-10-14, superseded 2026-10-18 by §5.5; the program survives as a candidate generator and as the experiment's descriptive reference):

```
maximize Σ_i σ_i w_i   s.t.  ‖C w‖₂ ≤ X_j,  μ_s'w ≥ Y,  1'w = 1,  min ≤ w ≤ max
```

a second-order cone program, the frontier's own program with another linear objective (cvxpy + Clarabel, DPP-parametrized, about 1 ms a solve), each solve a global optimum of its own program. Where the risk constraint does not bind the answer sits inside the ceiling with `σ(w) < X_j`; under the 2026-10-18 contract every mix does, so the `on_line` flag this paragraph once introduced is gone (§7) and the builder checks `σ(w) ≤ X_j + 1e-6` everywhere. Role from 2026-10-18: a Stage B measure of §5.5. The admissible set it is maximized over is bounded by sampled percentiles and is not convex, so the global-optimum property above belongs to the generator program, not to the selection; the selected mix is "the most diversified admissible mix found" for DR² exactly as for ENB. The generator's answers at several floors `Y` are cheap candidates that favour the DR² direction and join the column's pool (§5.5). Known behaviour to watch (demo spec F3): the ratio favours the least-correlated, higher-volatility funds (gold, long Treasuries, emerging markets); the caps and the §5.5 guards bound it (a return floor no longer does), and the single-fund and bound checks of §10 are the test.

### 5.3 Effective number of loss contributors in bad years, ENLC

From the selected engine's paths of a mix, using the exact decomposition of §4.5. The contribution of fund `n` to path `p`'s additive year is

```
C_{p,n} = Σ_t w_n [ m_n + k_w (R'_{p,t,n} − R̄'_n) ] = w_n [ 252 m_n + k_w (S_{p,n} − 252 R̄'_n) ],   S_{p,n} = Σ_t R'_{p,t,n}
```

so that `Σ_n C_{p,n} = Σ_t y'_{p,t}`. `S` (`P × N`) does not depend on the mix and is computed once per (fund set, engine, lookback); a mix's contributions are then a `P × N` product, and only the bad-year set below needs the mix's own paths, which the range computation already produces.

The mix's bad years are the `⌈αP⌉ = 100` paths with the lowest compounded return `Y_p`, `α = 5%` **[choice]** (the worst 100 of 2,000, the tail the Bad year sits in; the conditional average over them is the mix's expected shortfall at 5%). Fund `n`'s loss contribution is `ℓ_n = −mean_{p ∈ bad} C_{p,n}`, positive when the fund loses in the mix's bad years. Shares over the losers only, `s_n = max(ℓ_n, 0) / Σ_k max(ℓ_k, 0)`, and

```
ENLC(w) = exp(−Σ_n s_n ln s_n)        (0 ln 0 = 0)
```

Rule for negative contributions: a fund with `ℓ_n < 0` gained in the mix's bad years; it is a hedge, takes share 0, and is listed by name ("gained in this mix's bad years: TSP G"). Its gain already shows in the Bad-year figure; the count measures how many separate sources the losses come from. A fund with `w_n = 0` has `C = 0`. If no fund loses (`Σ max(ℓ, 0) = 0`: a cash-only mix, or TSP G or SGOV held alone, whose worst years are still gains), ENLC is undefined: the tensor stores `null` and the page says "no losses to share out" and falls back to the covariance measure for the word. The three largest shares, descending, with their fund ids are stored as `top`. Single fund: exactly 1 whenever its bad years lose, `null` otherwise. Bound: the number of funds with a positive loss contribution, at most the number held. Engine-dependent by construction: it is the only candidate that sees joint tails, which is what the owner wants measured; under `copula` it reflects fitted tail dependence, under `garch` the history's joint residual days, under `replay` the lookback's joint days. **[choice]** exp(entropy) rather than `1 / Σ s²` so the three measures are all Hill numbers of order 1 and compare like for like. Monte Carlo error: 100 tail paths give noisy shares; the shared draw makes neighbouring mixes comparable (their bad-year sets overlap almost entirely) and the seed-stability test of §10 bounds the error on the figure itself. Cost: negligible beyond the ranges (the bad-year set comes from the mix's compounded years, the rest is a `P × N` product). Optimization: not tractable directly. The function is sampled and piecewise (the bad-year set changes discretely with `w`); selecting a candidate's bad set needs its `2,000 × 252` series, about 5 ms, and 2,000 programs with numerical gradients in 23 dimensions would run for hours. It is measured on optimized mixes, never optimized, in this spec; re-ranking a pool of optimized candidates by ENLC (a few thousand evaluations, 10 to 20 s per lookback) is the later option in decision 2, §13.

**The objective: a two-stage preference contract (owner decision D1, §3 item 7; supersedes §3 item 5's "without inventing weights" and the fixed-risk ladder design).** For a risk ceiling `X` (a column `X_j`), an engine `e` and the shared draw of §4.1, with `B(w)`, `T(w)`, `G(w)` the unrounded q2.5 / q50 / q97.5 of the mix's compounded years in decimal units (1 bp = 0.0001):

```
F(X) = { w : min ≤ w ≤ max,  1'w = 1,  σ(w) ≤ X }                      (a ceiling, never an equality)
U(w) = 0.60 B(w) + 0.30 T(w) + 0.10 G(w)
Filter    remove from the pool every candidate dominated on (B, T, G): no quantile lower at all, one higher by > 1e-10
Stage A   w_R = argmax_{w ∈ F(X)} U(w) over the filtered pool;   B_R, T_R, G_R, U_R its figures
A(a)  = { w ∈ F(X) : B ≥ B_R − 10 bp,  T ≥ T_R − 25 bp,  G ≥ G_R − 50 bp,  U ≥ U_R − a,
                     not (B < B_R − 1e-10  and  T < T_R − 1e-10) }       (admissible at allowance a; the veto last)
Stage B   w_M(a) = argmax_{w ∈ A(a)} M(w),   M = DR² or ENB (the variant's measure)
```

The weights encode the importance order Bad > Typical > Good with finite trade-offs: one bp of Bad is worth two of Typical or six of Good, so for the owner's example `U(B) − U(A) = +1.794 pp` and B wins; there is no kink or target at Bad = 0, and improving an already-positive Bad counts like any other improvement. Every guard bounds a **reduction** only; improvements in any outcome are uncapped and never count against a candidate. `w_R` always passes (`w_R ∈ A(a)` for every `a`), and `A(a) ⊆ A(a')` when `a ≤ a'`. The dominance filter runs before either stage: a candidate dominates another only when no return quantile is lower at all and at least one is higher by more than `1e-10`, a strict partial order that prevents tolerance-induced cycles; `1e-10` is numerical resolution, not economic materiality; a dominated candidate cannot be selected however high its measure. The measure plays no part in Stage A, so `w_R` and `A(a)` are the same for both variants under one engine; only Stage B differs. The cap on `a` is 2.5 bp of `U` (primary); §6 maps the slider onto `[0, cap]`. Ties (protocol §2, owner policy 2026-10-18): for each ranking, first compute its global maximum, then form the tie set within `1e-12` of that maximum; Stage A selects from its tie set by higher `B`, then `T`, then `G`, then the lexicographically smaller weight vector in fixed menu order; Stage B by higher `U`, then `B`, `T`, `G`, then the same weight order; never pairwise approximate-tie comparisons, so no selection depends on the order candidates were visited. `B`, `T`, `G` are the selected engine's, so `w_R`, the admissible set and the ladder are per engine (§6, §7); the two measures are engine-independent functions of `w`, the selections that use them are not.

"Found" is the honest label for both stages, as it was for the fixed-risk ENB ladder. Stage A's objective is a sampled, piecewise-constant function of `w` (percentiles of 2,000 compounded paths; §5.3 says the same of ENLC), so it is searched within a budget, not solved to a certified optimum; Stage B's admissible set is bounded by the same sampled percentiles and is not convex, so the DR² SOCP's global-optimum property (§5.2) belongs to its role as a candidate generator, not to the selection. Every candidate evaluated in a column (Stage A's starts and iterates, Stage B's search at every allowance level, the frontier mix, the fixed-risk programs of §5.1 and §5.2 used as generators; the MDP at the ceiling is one of the shared anchors, §3 item 7 D5) joins the column's **pool** with its exact `B/T/G/U`, `ES_2.5` and both measures on the shared draw; the level at allowance `a_l` is the pool's highest-measure member of `A(a_l)`. By construction, down a ladder the measure never falls and `U` never falls below `U_R − a_l`; the tensor's note says "the most diversified admissible mix found", and the search budget per column is a build parameter recorded in the tensor (§7) and measured against §9. ## 10. Validation plan: when does a new engine become the default?

All of it runs on the laptop against the backup mirror (`LocalStore` over `/mnt/d/portfoliosight-data`), with no provider calls, by `scripts/backtest_ranges.py`, and is written up in `docs/reviews/2026-10-xx_engine-validation.md` with its tables. The owner reads it and decides (§13, decision 12).

1. **Rolling out-of-sample coverage.** Menus: Example Portfolio (23 funds) and TSP (5). Origins: the last trading day of each quarter from 2015-12-31 to 2025-09-30 (40 origins; the realized year of the last ends by 2026-09-30). At each origin the engine builds with `D_G` = origin, applying its own window and inclusion rules as they would have applied then (the shape window is what existed, down to the 252-row minimum). Mixes: frontier nodes at `s_safe`, the domain midpoint and `s_max`; the Optimized point of those columns; the entered mix; equal weight; every single fund. Lookbacks `5y` (the default) and `1y` (where level and shape differ most). For each (origin, mix, engine): predicted Bad / Typical / Good; realized one-year total return over the next 252 trading days. Metrics: coverage of `[Bad, Good]` (target 95%) and the breach count below Bad (target 2.5%), reported descriptively for the 40 quarterly origins (they overlap and cluster) and for the 10 non-overlapping year-end origins, with the note that `Binomial(10, 0.025)` puts 90% of its mass on 0 or 1 breaches, so a count cannot discriminate engines on its own; pinball loss at 2.5 / 50 / 97.5, summed over the mixes of an origin and paired engine against engine origin by origin; a Kolmogorov–Smirnov statistic of the PIT (the realized return's percentile under the engine's simulated distribution) on the 10 non-overlapping origins pooled over mixes, as a diagnostic. Because the level (`μ_s`, `σ`) is common to every engine, differences between engines are shape differences, while the absolute coverage also tests the menu-beta level. Honesty note: ten independent years is a weak test; the report says what it can and cannot distinguish, and the mixes of one origin are not independent evidence. **Harness, 2026-10-18 (D6):** `scripts/backtest_ranges.py` is written once for both this validation and the experiment: the same origins, the same "nothing after the origin" rule for fitting, selection and preference tuning, and the same realized-outcome legs; the experiment adds its arms, banks and seeds as subcommands. Its leakage check is metamorphic: altering any price strictly after an origin changes that origin's realized outcomes and changes nothing it predicted or selected (`w_R`, each arm's selection at every allowance the harness evaluates, the engine's percentiles). The harness produces arm selections at the evaluated allowances, not the 14-level product ladder, which only a build publishes. The mix set gains `w_R` and the arm's selection at the cap allowance at the three named columns under both measures.
2. **Engine comparison on the live menus** (current `D_G`): the Bad-year figures of the three engines at every frontier node and Optimized point of both menus, side by side; and the percentile at which each stress window's buy-and-hold loss (the three windows of the demo spec §4.10) falls in each engine's one-year distribution for the Optimized point at `s_safe`, the midpoint and `s_max`. A window shorter than a year is a lower bound on how bad that year could have been, and the report says so. An engine that places the 2008 outcome of an equity-heavy mix far below its 0.5th percentile is too thin in the tail; one that places 2022's loss of a 60/40 mix above its 5th percentile is too fat. Both the lookback and the ten-year shape window end after 2016, so the 2008 placement tests extrapolation beyond the fitted history, which is the point.
3. **Single-fund and bound checks of every measure**, both menus, every lookback and both variants: single-fund mixes give `DR² = 1` (1e-9) and `ENLC = 1` exactly for every fund whose bad years lose (`null` with the "no losses to share out" diagnostic otherwise), and ENB is reported as the finding. A null ENLC leaves the selected ENB/DR² word and optimizer in force; it does not silently choose DR². Every ladder level's `DR²` is at most the number of funds it holds (`w > 1e-6`, the §4.11 tolerance; the > 0.5% count is reported beside it) whenever the correlations among those funds are all ≥ 0, and every level's `ENLC` is at most the funds it holds.
4. **Seed stability.** The same build with five seeds (`20261013` to `20261017`): across seeds, at the tested points of A-SE-23 (every frontier node, each column's `w_R` under each engine, and the cap end of the sacrifice ladder under each measure (both variants); the seed-`20261013` weights are re-evaluated under the other seeds, never re-selected), the standard deviation of Bad is at most `0.10 · σ_w` (the 2.5th percentile's Monte Carlo error is about `0.06 σ_w` at 2,000 paths, §9, so this allows for fatter simulated tails) and the standard deviation of ENLC is at most 0.3; these are estimates of the error, not bounds. If the ENLC half fails, `α` moves to 10% (decision 5) and the test is rerun.
5. **Invariants and determinism** (unit tests): every engine's nondegenerate ranges have Bad < Typical < Good, with Typical within `0.25σ` of `e^{μ − σ²/2} − 1`, and `ES_2.5 ≤ Bad`. **Owner clarification, 2026-10-17:** for an exactly constant portfolio, Bad = Typical = Good (and `ES_2.5`) equals its compounded return; do not jitter the figures or reject the portfolio to force strict ordering. Level 0's range is `w_R`'s (the node's under D3 option i); identical tensors from identical inputs; fallbacks exercised on a TSP-G-like constant series, a 200-row series and a non-stationary synthetic series; the splice guard on a fixture with a fabricated 30% ex-date drop.
6. **Preference-contract oracles and the default-landing regression (2026-10-18, §5.5).** Unit oracles that never call the production selector, on hand-built `B/T/G` tables and on small enumerable menus with a fixed shared draw:
   - *Owner example.* `U(B) − U(A) = +0.01794` for A `[−0.0500, 0.04, 0.12]`, B `[−0.0501, 0.08, 0.18]`; B is selected over A as `w_R`; a strict-lexicographic rule, which would pick A, fails.
   - *Guard direction.* A candidate above `w_R` on every quantile is admissible at allowance 0 only if `U ≥ U_R` (it then is `w_R` or its tie); a candidate below `w_R` by exactly the guard on one quantile and above on the others is admissible; below by the guard plus `1e-9` on any one quantile is not. Guards are compared to `w_R`'s unrounded figures, never to a rounded or displayed value.
   - *Veto.* A candidate with Bad and Typical both lower than `w_R`'s by more than `1e-10`, a higher Good that keeps `U` within the allowance, and a higher measure, is inadmissible at every allowance; one lower on Bad only or Typical only, within its guard, is admissible.
   - *Cap cases.* The same pool selected at caps 0, 2.5, 5 and 10 bp: at 0 the selection is `w_R`; the selected measure is non-decreasing and the selected `U` non-increasing in the cap; `sacrifice ≤ allowance` at every level; the admissible sets are nested.
   - *Two-stage selection.* Under one engine, level 0 of the `enb` and `dr2` ladders is the same mix at every column; changing the measure changes no `w_R`, no `A(a)`, and no level-0 figure; Stage A ignores the measure even when the highest-measure feasible mix has a higher `U` than every Stage A start (the search must find it through `U`, not through the measure).
   - *Cycle fixture.* `A = (0, 0, 0)`, `B = (1.5e, −0.75e, −0.75e)`, `C = (0.75e, 0.75e, −1.5e)`, `e = 1e-10`, in every input order: the dominance relation is acyclic and the tie set is formed against the global maximum within `1e-12`, not by pairwise tolerances.
   - *Default-landing regression.* The fixture of §5.5: TSP, 5y, the saved artifact `2026-10-16-0.2.0/63643e52f0386317/5y.json` and its successor built under the contract, at the default landing position (the column whose ceiling is 9.1%) and, separately, at the investigation position `r = 36.382658` (≈ 12.53% risk). The default landing mix (`d` absent, `d=auto`, `d=0`) equals `w_R` at every column of both presets and every lookback, and at interpolated positions equals the blend of the two columns' `w_R`; its exact Bad and Typical are therefore never both below `w_R`'s. The report prints, beside it, the frontier mix's figures at the same ceiling and the old default's (−10.5 / +7.4 against −7.9 / +8.2): if the new default is below the frontier mix on both Bad and Typical at any column, that is a finding for C8 (the contract's own optimum can be, when Good carries `U`), printed with its figures, not a reason to move the default.
   - *`ES_2.5`.* `ES_2.5` equals the independent mean of the 50 lowest compounded years on the consumed paths; `≤ Bad`; equal to Bad = Typical = Good on a constant portfolio; a single fund's `ES_2.5` under `replay` equals the same computation on its own resampled years; no guard, `w_R` or ladder level changes when `ES_2.5` is perturbed (it is read by nothing in §5.5).
   - *DR² covariance (D5).* DR² from the tensor's `sigma` and `cov` equals the formula on the production estimator's output; on a fixture whose held funds' pairwise correlations are all non-negative, DR² from the shrunk covariance is at least DR² from the raw sample covariance for the same long-only weights (DR² rises with correlation shrinkage toward the identity for long-only holdings whose pairwise correlations are non-negative; a negatively correlated pair can move the other way); on a fixture holding a negatively correlated pair, the sign of the difference is reported, not asserted; adding weight to an exactly-zero-volatility fund (renormalizing the rest) leaves DR² unchanged to `1e-12`.
7. **Which measure optimizes better (scope reconciled after C8).** The paired ENB-against-DR² realized comparison as first written here (Optimized point and peak of each column under the fixed-risk ladders) is superseded by the experiment protocol and D4: the arms are the return baseline, +DR² and, if gated in, +ENB; the comparison points are `w_R` and the cap end of each column's sacrifice ladder, reported beside the three return-agnostic references at the same ceiling (minimum variance, cap-projected equal weight and the MDP of §3 item 7 D5), against which R is validated before R+D versus R is interpreted; the selection rule is §5.5. The realized metrics stay as reporting requirements: realized one-year volatility against the ceiling (absolute error, and the share of years realized risk exceeded the ceiling by more than a quarter); the realized loss in each stress window and in the worst realized year; realized loss concentration (ENLC on the realized bad days, §5.3's definition); realized return given up against the frontier mix of the same column; stability (the weight distance between the same column's mixes at consecutive origins, and between lookbacks at one origin). The default measure changes only on "confirmed worthwhile benefit" (realized benefit support in both menus and no material-harm risk cell, protocol §7) and the owner's explicit decision; a tie keeps ENB. The report shows a table per menu and a one-paragraph verdict.
8. **Decision rule proposed to the owner.** An engine may become the default when, on both menus and both lookbacks tested: its paired pinball loss at 2.5% (summed over mixes) is lower than `replay`'s in at least 8 of the 10 non-overlapping years (a sign test at about 5%) and its total pinball loss at the 50th and 97.5th percentiles is no worse; its breach count on the 10 years is 0 or 1 where `replay`'s is not better; it places no stress outcome (item 2) below its 0.5th percentile where `replay` did not; items 3 to 6 pass; and the owner's own check of the page (Expectations) passes. Ten years can only show large differences; a tie on evidence leaves the default where it is unless the owner decides on the method's merits. `copula` is judged against `garch` the same way. The default switch itself is one constant (`range_default`) and one engine-version bump. Because the holdings follow the engine from 2026-10-18, an engine default switch also changes the default holdings; the report shows `w_R` under both engines at the three named columns before the owner decides.

## 12. Risks

- **The fit library.** `arch` fits can land on boundary values (`α + β` near 1, `ν` near 2) or fail to converge on short or odd series; the fallback is constant volatility, flagged, never silent. `arch` is pinned exactly because its optimizer's results decide the tensor's bytes.
- **Short overlap pools.** A menu holding a young fund has a pool as short as the window; joint tails are then poorly identified and the copula fit is unstable. The tensor states the pool length; the details show it; the report checks the Example Portfolio's and TSP's pools (both long).
- **Level and shape from different regimes, by design.** A 1-year view takes a fund's level from a calm year and its shape from ten years including 2020 and 2022. That is the owner's intent (the shape is the fund's character); the bar and the readouts will be wider than `replay`'s in calm years, and the report shows by how much.
- **DR² preferences.** The ratio rewards the least-correlated higher-volatility funds; with the caps and the §5.5 guards it stays within the demo's limits, and F3 (one fund dominating) is checked by the owner on the page.
- **Sacrifice-ladder build cost (2026-10-18).** The objective costs a `P × H` compounding per candidate per engine; §9 shows that a modest search budget already exceeds the 30 s build budget at 23 funds and two engines. The levers are named in §9; M6 removes the obvious one. If the measured cost does not fit, the choice between fewer solved columns and a longer budget is the owner's (§13 item 23), not a silent reduction in paths.
- **Engine-dependent holdings (2026-10-18).** Under the contract the selected mix follows the engine, so an engine switch moves the selected point, the ladder and the map (§6). S3's "the chart itself does not move" is narrowed and the owner is asked to confirm (§13 item 22); A-SE-12's no-geometry-change leg holds for Phase 1 tensors only.
- **Old links (2026-10-18).** `d=auto` and numeric `d` values in links shared before this change now denote `w_R` and an allowance; the page shows the mix the address now means, with no notice **[choice]** (the address is the state; a notice about a retired meaning would outlive its usefulness).
